## Building a Davos Assistant on Claude
Time: 00:00 - 01:15

Host: Opens by thanking Anthropic—she built a Davos assistant on Claude that holds her calendar and notes, does not get jet-lagged, and costs no lodging. She asks how many in the audience are Claude power users; many raise their hands.

Host: Frames the conversation as a “game”: she will list product wishes and Dario can say whether each is coming, when, or not at all.

## Consumer Roadmap: Web, Voice, Images, Rate Limits, Memory
Time: 01:15 - 05:03

Host: First wish—web access.

Dario Amodei: Web is in active development and coming relatively soon. Anthropic is mostly enterprise-focused, so enterprise features often ship first; web is more consumer-facing but they have been working on it and believe their approach will differ from other providers. He will not give an exact date but says it is prioritized for consumers and power users.

Host: Second—two-way voice mode.

Dario Amodei: Eventually yes. Claude can already transcribe speech and read text aloud, but full duplex voice is planned. Again, lower enterprise demand, so timing is later.

Host: Photo / image generation.

Dario Amodei: He treats image and video generation as somewhat separate from core generative AI—humans ingest media but do not naturally “generate” photos the way models do. Safety issues are also distinct. Low enterprise use case; not a priority. If consumers need it, Anthropic may partner with specialists rather than build in-house.

Host: Higher rate limits—top request from her X poll.

Dario Amodei: They are working hard on capacity. Demand surged over the last year, especially the last three months, outpacing compute supply; large compute purchases have lead times. Revenue grew roughly 10× in a year (order of $100M to ~$1B) and is not slowing. They are improving efficiency and scaling Trainium 2 with Amazon; he would not be surprised if by 2026 they have more than a million chips of some kind. Experience should improve even for paying users as capacity comes online.

Host: Cross-project memory.

Dario Amodei: Important and part of the “virtual collaborator” vision—a coworker remembers past conversations. Coming soon.

## Why Claude's Character Matters Everywhere
Time: 05:03 - 08:20

Host: As a consumer she keeps returning to Claude because of its character; how does that translate beyond enterprise?

Dario Amodei: Character matters on both sides—consumer chat, enterprise customer service (e.g. Intercom), and even coding. A recent Stanford medical study found doctors adopted Claude’s radiology suggestions more than other models’, partly due to interaction style. The goal is not just engagement but long-term benefit: after months or years you should be more productive and learn, not trapped in a dopamine loop like social media. That lesson shapes how they design Claude.

## Reasoning Models as a Continuum
Time: 08:20 - 10:03

Host: Power users want the next models; OpenAI has o1/o3-style reasoning—what is Anthropic’s plan?

Dario Amodei: Strong new models are coming in the not-too-distant future (no names). They see “reasoning” and test-time compute as a spectrum, not a separate species of model. Sonnet 3.5 already reflects somewhat; more reinforcement learning at scale should make models think and reflect more as an emergent property. They may blend reasoning with other capabilities more fluidly than competitors.

## No Dates on Stage—and Models in 3–6 Months
Time: 10:03 - 11:00

Host: Presses for timing on new models.

Dario Amodei: He will not give dates at this event. When she pins “not so distant future,” he allows “between 3 and 6 months” for model releases.

## Is Progress Really This Fast?
Time: 11:00 - 15:05

Host: Industry chatter says progress is breakneck—e.g. reports of OpenAI’s “PhD-level agent.” Is it?

Dario Amodei: Describes his own epistemic journey. Since documenting scaling laws (~2017–2018) with Anthropic co-founders, he suspected models would eventually beat almost all humans at almost everything, likely mid-2020s, but always hedged—CEOs should not sound like hype men. Until roughly 3–6 months ago uncertainty was large; it is much reduced now. Over the next 2–3 years he is relatively confident workplace and consumer models will gradually exceed humans at almost everything, with great upside and real risks. Progress is as fast as people think; the industry must communicate with gravity, not sly Twitter winks that make outsiders dismiss seriousness.

## Virtual Collaborators—not Clippy++
Time: 15:05 - 18:45

Host: Davos is “agent palooza”; Anthropic says “virtual collaborators.” Roadmap after computer use?

Dario Amodei: Terms like “agent,” AGI, ASI, and “reasoning” lack precise technical meaning—agents range from Clippy++ to systems that spin up full apps. Anthropic’s target is closer to the latter: a model that can do on a screen what a virtual human could—code, test, deploy, write docs, Slack, email—over hours or a day, checking in periodically. Computer use is an early ingredient. He suspects (not promises) a strong version could land this year, possibly first half.

## AI and Work: Complementarity Now, Hard Questions Later
Time: 18:45 - 22:30

Host: That vision displaces many jobs people do today.

Dario Amodei: Split short vs long term. Short term (~1–3 years): historical labor disruption patterns apply—comparative advantage means even if AI does 90% of a job, humans can specialize in the remaining 10% at much higher leverage. Product design can favor complementarity over substitution; Erik Brynjolfsson’s work shows default “replacement” deployments vs thoughtful “complementary” ones yield bigger productivity gains. Anthropic tries to design the virtual collaborator that way, though brutal market efficiency could force substitution if complementarity loses competitively.

Long term: plausibly by ~2027 or not much later, AI may beat all humans at everything; robotics follows. Society must rethink economic organization and meaning when labor is no longer the main way to distribute abundance or self-worth. He fears uneven automation (30% vs 70%) more than everyone affected together—shared crisis forces collective solutions. AI companies will sit at the center and need answers.

## Talent Density and Living Values
Time: 22:30 - 25:06

Host: Meanwhile there is a war for talent vs OpenAI, Meta, etc.

Dario Amodei: Talent density beats talent mass—a smaller team where almost everyone is exceptional outperforms a larger firm with twice as many good people. Second, values must be substantive, not marketing; over time promises vs actions and where people “vote with their feet” reveal who is serious about safety.

## Policy Actor, Not Political Actor
Time: 25:06 - 28:15

Host: Notes he was not at the inauguration.

Dario Amodei: Anthropic is a policy actor, not a political actor—focused on the global AI landscape, not currying favor to avoid antitrust or regulation. They will articulate views (China, export controls, testing dangers, labor economics) to any administration—Biden before, Trump now. Example: op-ed with Matt Pottinger on bipartisan export-control interest. They will not declare fealty to one party; if their analysis is right, history should vindicate them within a presidential term.

## Export Controls, AISI Testing, and the Executive Order
Time: 28:15 - 30:07

Host: Reaction to overturning the Biden AI executive order that morning?

Dario Amodei: The order was a small first step—reporting on large training runs, roughly “one person-day” at Anthropic, not a heavy regime. Export controls and anti-smuggling to China are existential—military/intelligence value of models is emerging; a lead over China buys room to mitigate domestic risks without a Hobbesian race. AISI-style testing for national security risks (not DEI) has bipartisan support to continue.

## People, AI, and Marginal Returns to Intelligence
Time: 30:07 - 31:45

Host: Asks about his “one person day” phrase.

Dario Amodei: Increasingly they think in people plus models plus compute—complementary. His essay “Machines of Loving Grace” argues the economy should think about marginal returns to intelligence, not only land, labor, and capital—some tasks benefit hugely; others hit physical or societal limits.

## Cloud Giants, Capital, and Independence
Time: 31:45 - 34:15

Host: Amazon’s $4B and reported raises—confirm $2B?

Dario Amodei: Cannot confirm ongoing raises; total reported funding is well into double-digit billions. Partnerships with Amazon and Google make economic sense for compute and deployment security, but independence matters—multiple cloud partners, and contract terms aligned with Anthropic’s Responsible Scaling Policy, which has pushed some clouds toward similar practices—a “race to the top.”

## Career Advice in the AI Era
Time: 34:15 - 35:29

Host: Advice for high school, college, or early-career people?

Dario Amodei: Learn to use the tools as they evolve quickly. More importantly, cultivate critical thinking—the information ecosystem is scrambled by plausible AI text, images, and video; the old curated web is gone. Ask whether claims make sense; ideally use AI to strengthen critical thinking rather than further corrupt the ecosystem.
